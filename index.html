THE FIRST THREE QUESTIONS

Pulse and perfusion decide the algorithm

Ask in this order

  1. 1No pulse sends you to the cardiac arrest algorithm.
  2. 2Too fast or too slow decides tachyarrhythmia or bradycardia.
  3. 3Cardiopulmonary compromise decides how fast you must act.
Rhythm naming comes last

Cardiopulmonary compromise

  • Acutely altered mental status
  • Signs of shock
  • Hypotension

STABLE, NARROW COMPLEX

Stable SVT gets vagal maneuvers, then adenosine

Sequence

  1. 1Vagal maneuvers first, using ice to the face or a blocked straw.
  2. 2Adenosine 0.1 mg/kg, maximum 6 mg, rapid push with a flush.
  3. 3Repeat adenosine 0.2 mg/kg, maximum 12 mg.
Vagal maneuvers need no IV

Getting adenosine in

  • Use a large vein close to the heart
  • Push the drug and the flush together, fast
  • Run a rhythm strip through the whole dose
UNSTABLE, NARROW COMPLEX

Compromised SVT gets adenosine, then cardioversion

0.5 to 1 J/kg
Synchronized cardioversion, raised to 2 J/kg if ineffective
  • This is the probable-SVT branch, not every fast narrow rhythm
  • If IV or IO access is present, give adenosine
  • If there is no access, or adenosine fails, cardiovert
  • Sedate if you can, but do not delay the shock

TAKE HOME

What to carry back to your resuscitation room

  1. 1Check pulse and perfusion before naming the rhythm.
  2. 2Narrow or wide at 0.09 seconds sets the path.
  3. 3Adenosine 0.1 then 0.2 mg/kg, rapid push and flush.
  4. 4Cardiovert at 0.5 to 1, then 2 J/kg.
  5. 5Under 60 with poor perfusion after oxygen means compressions.
  6. 6Defibrillate at 2, then 4 J/kg and above.
